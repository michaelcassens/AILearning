What each mechanic is actually teaching

Resilience is not the absence of hard things. Timberkin's resilience meter only rises through supported challenge: practicing a coping skill that fits the feeling, letting the animal attempt something first with you nearby, or recovering after a rough stretch. A day with no challenge at all produces no growth.

Rescue has a cost. Handing over a treat to fix a feeling (rather than hunger) spikes mood, then drops it lower than before, and pushes the animal toward a "waiting to be rescued" state where it stops trying things on its own. Three of those in a row and resilience drops. This is deliberate: over-functioning for someone teaches them they can't.

Neglect has a different cost. Chronically unmet needs lead to a withdrawn state — not death. There is no game over. Repair takes patient, small, consistent care over time, which is how rupture and repair actually works.

Communication is the difficulty setting. At low Voice the animal can only emit a "!" and you must infer the need from posture, scene, and time of day. Every reflective-listening response raises Voice, and at high Voice it states its feeling and its need in a sentence, and begins asking for help unprompted. Teaching a creature to name what's wrong is what makes caring for it possible — the game makes that literal.

Fit matters more than effort. Each distress event has an underlying feeling. Breathing helps a racing alarm; it does very little for defeat. Movement discharges restless anger; it won't touch loneliness. Picking the wrong skill isn't punished, but the journal explains why it didn't land, which is how skill vocabulary gets built.

Sitting with it is a real option. "Sit With It" is always available and is sometimes the best move. Not every feeling is a problem to be solved.

Independence is the reward. Stock the cache, teach the skills, and the animal feeds itself, self-soothes, tidies up, and weathers your absence. High resilience means you can close the tab for three days and come back to a companion that kept its own fire going.

Suggested classroom use: set Speed to Classroom demo, run two devices side by side — one played "rescue every feeling with treats," one played "coach and practice" — and compare the resilience curves and the journals after a simulated week.

Something small is at the edge of the clearing

Pick a companion. It runs on your real clock, so it will get hungry, sleepy, and out of sorts whether you're watching or not — and it can learn to handle some of that itself, if you teach it how.

How to play

The eight icons around the screen are your actions. Press A to move between them and B to choose, like the original handheld — or just click an icon.

  • Feed — a real meal fills hunger. A treat is a quick mood bump with a price. Stock the cache lets your companion feed itself later.
  • Play — builds bond and burns restless energy.
  • Tidy — clean up messes. Left alone they drag wellbeing down.
  • Lights — your companion sleeps at night. Turn the lights off so it can.
  • Check in — the heart of the game. Notice, ask, listen. This is how Voice grows.
  • Skills — practice a coping skill together. Pick one that fits the feeling.
  • Vitals — the numbers, plus tree rings: one ring per day, colored by how that day went.
  • Notes — what your companion has learned and can now do alone.

There is no way to lose. There are better and worse ways to help.
