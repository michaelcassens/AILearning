Classes already teaching AI

Not just one program — AI shows up across Art & Media, Business, and Computer Science. Add rows here as other departments confirm more.

Three of these make up the Human-Centered AI certificate

Open to students across majors — the certificate stacks with an existing degree rather than replacing one.

See the course catalog
Course details reflect the current UM catalog and program pages — confirm term, section, and prerequisites before publishing.
CourseTitleWhat it coversContact
College of the Arts and Media — Human-Centered AI certificate
MART 464 Creatively Exploring AI: An Interdisciplinary Approach Highly experiential; open to any major. Understanding generative AI and using it effectively while weighing its ethical and moral dimensions. School of Art & Game Design
MART 474 Human-Centered Leadership in AI Leadership-focused follow-on course, part of the certificate sequence. School of Art & Game Design
JRNL 102Y Certificate elective Journalism-school contribution to the Human-Centered AI certificate. School of Journalism
College of Business
BGEN 341 People, Process and Technology I Required core course preparing business students for work shaped by automation, AI, and creative disruption — data-driven decision-making and the mindset it takes. College of Business
BGEN 342 People, Process and Technology II Continues the sequence: leading organizational change and becoming a confident, ethical technology decision-maker. College of Business
BGEN 541 Data Literacy Graduate Data's role in decision-making, plus the applications and ethical considerations of AI in business. businessgradprograms@umontana.edu
BGEN 620 Business Communication Graduate Written and verbal communication for business, including AI applications alongside frequently used communication technologies. businessgradprograms@umontana.edu
College of Science
CSCI 447 Machine Learning Learning from examples, algorithms such as neural networks, and principles like inductive bias and data mining. Offered autumn; co-convenes with CSCI 547 for graduate students. Computer Science Dept.

The College of Business also flags AI and machine learning as an elective add-on for its Master of Science in Business Analytics — worth a line here once a specific course is confirmed.
