Review Protocols

A protocol is a repeatable procedure you run on a real replay. Review without a procedure collapses into re-watching yourself play, which feels like work and teaches almost nothing. These unlock by depth, because each one assumes the ones below it are already habits.

Your single biggest advantage League has a replay system that most competitive games do not: you can watch the same game from all ten perspectives and toggle fog of war. That means you can literally see what the enemy jungler was doing while you were dying to him, and you can watch your own lane from the enemy laner's camera. Several protocols below are built specifically on that, and they are the closest thing to a cheat code in this entire module. Replays are stored locally and can be lost on patch changes, so download the ones you intend to study.

How It Works

The four tiers are a thinking ladder, not a rank ladder

Every question is tagged to a tier. The tier is not “how good you are at League” — it is what kind of cognitive work the question demands of you. A Diamond player who has never interrogated a habit can be stuck at Tier 1 thinking, and a Silver player who reasons carefully can operate at Tier 2. Mechanical rank and analytical depth are correlated, but they are not the same variable, and the gap between them is where most stalled climbs live.

The four tiers of analytical demand
TierDemandThe question sounds like
1 — IdentifyName what happened, and separate what you observed from what you concluded.“What actually killed me, as opposed to what I felt killed me?”
2 — ExplainTrace the cause through the systems of the game: wave state, vision, cooldowns, gold, timers.“What made that gank available thirty seconds before it happened?”
3 — EvaluateJudge a decision on the information available at the time, not on how it turned out.“Given what I could see then, was that the right trade — and could I have executed the better one?”
4 — ModelReason about your own reasoning, and about nine other players reasoning about you.“What is my model of this jungler, how would I know if it is wrong, and what does he think I am about to do?”

How the engine grows with you

  • Each of the nine tracks carries its own depth value from 1.0 to 4.0. Answering well pushes that track up; missing pulls it down. Depth falls faster than it climbs, which keeps you near the edge of what you can actually do rather than comfortably inside it.
  • The engine serves your weakest unmastered track most often, so your time goes where the return is highest — with a deliberate minority of questions from stronger tracks so they do not decay.
  • Questions are weighted toward your role. A support and a top laner get different versions of the same lesson, because the information each of them has, and the failures each of them owns, are genuinely different.
  • Mastery is capped by evidence. Proficient requires clearing a Tier 3 item. Mastered requires Tier 4. Grinding Tier 1 plateaus you at Familiar, which is the honest result.
  • Items you get wrong are returned to you later, not immediately — spaced by several questions, because re-answering something you just saw tests your short-term memory rather than your understanding.
  • Hints are a ladder: the first reframes the question, the second narrows the field, the third nearly answers it. Using hints reduces credit, because a hinted answer is weaker evidence that you can do it unaided.

Why the content avoids exact numbers

You will notice this module almost never quotes a cooldown, a damage value, a gold amount or a respawn timer. That is deliberate, and it is two things at once. League is patched every few weeks, so any number printed here would rot inside a season. More importantly, looking the number up yourself is the skill. A player who knows the enemy jungler's Smite is down because they watched him take a camp is doing analysis. A player who memorized a table is doing trivia. The durable content here is the reasoning; the numbers are homework you do against the current patch.

What this module cannot do

Nothing here grades your written answers — there is no model behind this page. Free-response items are scored by an explicit rubric that you apply to your own answer after reading an expert response. That is a real and well-evidenced technique (self-explanation against a worked example), and it works, but it runs on your honesty. Every item also has a Copy coaching prompt button that packages your answer, the scenario and your current depth into a prompt for an AI assistant that can read and respond to it. The AI Coach tab builds a fuller version of that.
