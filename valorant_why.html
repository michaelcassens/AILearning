How It Works

The four tiers are a thinking ladder, not a rank ladder

Every question is tagged to a tier. The tier is not “how good you are at Valorant” — it is what kind of cognitive work the question demands of you. An Immortal who has never interrogated a habit can be stuck at Tier 1 thinking, and a Silver player who reasons carefully can operate at Tier 2.

The four tiers of analytical demand
TierDemandThe question sounds like
1 — IdentifyName what happened, and separate what you observed from what you concluded.“What actually killed me, as opposed to what I felt killed me?”
2 — ExplainTrace the cause through the systems of the game: space, utility, credits, timing, information.“What made that round unwinnable twenty seconds before the fight?”
3 — EvaluateJudge a decision on the information available at the time, not on how it turned out.“Given what I could see and hear then, was there a better option — and could I have executed it?”
4 — ModelReason about your own reasoning, and about five opponents reasoning about you.“What is my model of this team, how would I know if it is wrong, and what do they think I am about to do?”

How the engine grows with you

  • Each of the eight tracks carries its own depth value from 1.0 to 4.0. Answering well pushes that track up; missing pulls it down. Depth falls faster than it climbs, which keeps you near the edge of what you can actually do rather than comfortably inside it.
  • The engine serves your weakest unmastered track most often, so your time goes where the return is highest — with a deliberate minority of questions from stronger tracks so they do not decay. You can override that from the Progress tab and drill one track on purpose.
  • Questions are weighted toward your role. A Controller and a Duelist get different versions of the same lesson, because the information each of them has, and the failures each of them owns, are different.
  • Mastery is capped by evidence. Proficient requires clearing a Tier 3 item. Mastered requires Tier 4. Grinding Tier 1 plateaus you at Familiar, which is the honest result.
  • Items you get wrong are returned to you later, not immediately — spaced by several questions, because re-answering something you just saw tests your short-term memory rather than your understanding.
  • Hints are a ladder: the first reframes the question, the second narrows the field, the third nearly answers it. Using hints reduces credit, because a hinted answer is weaker evidence that you can do it unaided.

Why the content avoids exact numbers

You will notice this module almost never quotes a damage value, a credit cost, an ability duration or a lineup. That is deliberate, and it is two things at once. Valorant is patched frequently and agents get reworked, so any number printed here would rot. More importantly, looking the number up yourself is the skill. A player who knows the enemy Controller has no smokes left for the retake — because they counted — is doing analysis. A player who memorized a wiki page is doing trivia. The durable content is the reasoning; the numbers are homework you do against the current patch.

What this module cannot do

Nothing here grades your written answers — there is no model behind this page. Free-response items are scored by an explicit rubric that you apply to your own answer after reading an expert response. That is a real and well-evidenced technique (self-explanation against a worked example), and it works, but it runs on your honesty. Every item also has a Copy coaching prompt button that packages your answer, the scenario and your current depth into a prompt for an AI assistant that can read and respond to it. The AI Coach tab builds a fuller version of that.
