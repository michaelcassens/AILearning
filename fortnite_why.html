Review Protocols

A protocol is a repeatable procedure you run on real footage. Review without a procedure collapses into re-watching yourself play, which feels like work and teaches almost nothing. These unlock by depth, because each one assumes the ones below it have become habits.

Fortnite gives you an unusually good instrument The in-game replay system records the whole match and lets you fly a free camera, follow any player, and scrub time. Almost no other competitive game hands players that. It means you can watch the person who killed you play the thirty seconds before they killed you — which is the single most educational thirty seconds available to you, and almost nobody watches it. Replays are tied to the build they were recorded on and are wiped by updates, so save or review anything interesting before the next patch.

How It Works

The four tiers are a thinking ladder, not a rank ladder

Every question is tagged to a tier. The tier is not “how good you are at Fortnite” — it is what kind of cognitive work the question demands of you. An Unreal player who has never interrogated a habit can be stuck at Tier 1 thinking, and a Gold player who reasons carefully can operate at Tier 2.

The four tiers of analytical demand
TierDemandThe question sounds like
1 — IdentifyName what happened, and separate what you observed from what you concluded.“What actually killed me, as opposed to what I felt killed me?”
2 — ExplainTrace the cause through the systems of the game: zone, resources, sound, sightlines, timing.“What made that fight unwinnable ninety seconds before it started?”
3 — EvaluateJudge a decision on the information available at the time, not on how it turned out — which in a battle royale is mostly noise.“Given what I could see then, was that rotation right even though I died?”
4 — ModelReason about your own reasoning, and about the other players reasoning about you.“What is my model of the remaining eight, how would I know if it is wrong, and what do they think I am about to do?”

How the engine grows with you

  • Each of the nine tracks carries its own depth value from 1.0 to 4.0. Answering well pushes that track up; missing pulls it down. Depth falls faster than it climbs, which keeps you near the edge of what you can actually do rather than comfortably inside it.
  • The engine serves your weakest unmastered track most often, so your time goes where the return is highest — with a deliberate minority of questions from stronger tracks so they do not decay.
  • Questions are weighted toward your mode. Build and Zero Build ask genuinely different questions about the same situation, because in one of them the terrain is something you make and in the other it is something you find.
  • Mastery is capped by evidence. Proficient requires clearing a Tier 3 item. Mastered requires Tier 4. Grinding Tier 1 plateaus you at Familiar, which is the honest result.
  • Items you get wrong are returned to you later, not immediately — spaced by several questions, because re-answering something you just saw tests your short-term memory rather than your understanding.
  • Hints are a ladder: the first reframes the question, the second narrows the field, the third nearly answers it. Using hints reduces credit, because a hinted answer is weaker evidence that you can do it unaided.

Why the content avoids exact numbers

You will notice this module almost never quotes a damage value, a storm timer, a material cap or a weapon rarity. That is deliberate, and it is two things at once. Fortnite is patched constantly and its loot pool is rewritten every season, so any number printed here would be wrong within weeks. More importantly, looking the number up yourself is the skill. A player who knows how long the current storm phase gives them — because they checked it this season — is doing analysis. A player who memorized a number from a video made two chapters ago is doing trivia, and doing it wrong.

Why a battle royale needs a different kind of review

Fortnite has a structural problem no arena game has: you get one life, the sample is enormous, and the loot is partly random. That means a single match is close to worthless as evidence, and your feelings about a match are actively misleading — the games you remember are the ones with the most dramatic endings, not the most informative ones. Nearly every protocol here therefore either forces you to look at a specific short window with a procedure, or forces you to aggregate across many games. Both exist for the same reason: to get a signal out of a very noisy channel.

What this module cannot do

Nothing here grades your written answers — there is no model behind this page. Free-response items are scored by an explicit rubric that you apply to your own answer after reading an expert response. That is a real and well-evidenced technique (self-explanation against a worked example), and it works, but it runs on your honesty. Every item also has a Copy coaching prompt button that packages your answer, the scenario and your current depth into a prompt for an AI assistant that can read and respond to it. The AI Coach tab builds a fuller version of that.
