How to use these Do not read all nine. Read the one matching your weakest skill, answer five questions in that skill, then come back and read it again. Reading a brief before you have failed at the skill is close to useless; reading it after you have failed is where it lands.

Review Protocols

A protocol is a repeatable procedure you run on real footage. Review without a procedure collapses into re-watching yourself play, which feels like work and teaches almost nothing. These unlock by depth, because each one assumes the ones below it are already habits.

Before you start Overwatch 2 gives you an unusually good analysis tool: the replay viewer, with every player's perspective, a free camera and a scrubbable timeline. Most of these protocols assume you can open a replay and move around inside it. Save replay codes for matches worth reviewing before they age out, and keep instant-replay capture running (Game Bar, GeForce or AMD, OBS) for the moments you want to keep permanently.

How It Works

The four tiers are a thinking ladder, not a rank ladder

Every question is tagged to a tier. The tier is not “how good you are at Overwatch” — it is what kind of cognitive work the question demands of you. A Grandmaster who has never interrogated a habit can be stuck at Tier 1 thinking, and a Gold player who reasons carefully can operate at Tier 2.

The four tiers of analytical demand
TierDemandThe question sounds like
1 — IdentifyName what happened, and separate what you observed from what you concluded.“What actually killed me, as opposed to what I felt killed me?”
2 — ExplainTrace the cause through the systems of the game: space, cooldowns, ultimate economy, timing.“What made that fight unwinnable fifteen seconds before it started?”
3 — EvaluateJudge a decision on the information available at the time, not on how it turned out.“Given what I could see then, was there a better option — and could I have executed it?”
4 — ModelReason about your own reasoning, and about five opponents reasoning about you.“What is my model of this enemy team, how would I know if it is wrong, and what do they think I am about to do?”

How the engine grows with you

  • Each of the nine skills carries its own depth value from 1.0 to 4.0. Answering well pushes that skill up; missing pulls it down. Depth falls faster than it climbs, which keeps you near the edge of what you can actually do rather than comfortably inside it.
  • The engine serves your weakest unmastered skill most often, so your time goes where the return is highest — with a deliberate minority of questions from stronger skills so they do not decay.
  • Questions are weighted toward your role. A Tank and a Support get different versions of the same lesson, because the information each of them has, and the failures each of them owns, are different.
  • Mastery is capped by evidence. Proficient requires clearing a Tier 3 item. Mastered requires Tier 4 and a passed mastery challenge. Grinding Tier 1 plateaus you at Familiar, which is the honest result.
  • Mastery challenges interleave four questions from four different skills, at your current depth, with hints switched off. Mixed retrieval is a much harder and much better test than answering five questions in a row about the same thing.
  • Items you get wrong are returned to you later, not immediately — spaced by several questions, because re-answering something you just saw tests your short-term memory rather than your understanding.
  • Hints are a ladder: the first reframes the question, the second narrows the field, the third nearly answers it. Using hints reduces credit, because a hinted answer is weaker evidence that you can do it unaided.

Why the content avoids exact numbers

You will notice this module almost never quotes a cooldown, a damage value, a health pool or an ultimate cost. That is deliberate, and it is two things at once. Overwatch 2 is balanced continuously, and hero perks, bans and mode rules have all changed inside single years — any number printed here would rot. More importantly, looking the number up yourself is the skill. A player who knows the enemy support's escape is down for roughly the next few seconds, because they counted it, is doing analysis. A player who memorized a wiki page is doing trivia. The durable content is the reasoning; the numbers are homework you do against the live patch.

What this module cannot do

Nothing here grades your written answers — there is no model behind this page. Free-response items are scored by an explicit rubric that you apply to your own answer after reading an expert response. That is a real and well-evidenced technique (self-explanation against a worked example), and it works, but it runs on your honesty. Every item also has a Copy coaching prompt button that packages your answer, the scenario and your current depth into a prompt for an AI assistant that can read and respond to it. The AI Coach tab builds a fuller version of that.
